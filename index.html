How Second Look works

Second Look looks for the tactics scams rely on: pressure (act now, your account is locked), an unusual request (gift cards, crypto, codes, remote access), and links that pretend to be someone else. Each warning sign is highlighted in your message and explained in one or two sentences.

What it checks in links

  • Where the link really goes. In paypal.com.account-help.net, the real site is account-help.net.
  • Look-alike spellings (paypa1, arnazon) and disguised characters.
  • Links that don’t match the company the message claims to be from.
  • Shortened links, raw number addresses and “@” tricks.

Your privacy

Everything runs in your browser. The page’s security policy blocks it from sending data anywhere, so even we can’t see what you paste. There are no accounts, no tracking and no analytics, and it keeps working without a connection.

What it can’t do

  • It can’t visit links or know whether a specific website is dangerous right now.
  • It can’t recognise every scam. New tricks appear all the time, so “no warning signs” is not a guarantee.
  • It doesn’t replace your bank, your provider or the police. If you’ve already paid or shared details, contact your bank straight away.
